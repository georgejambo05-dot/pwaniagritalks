What does sustainable agriculture mean?

It means producing food and income while caring for soil, water, and the farm's ability to keep producing over time. The right practices depend on local soil, rainfall, slope, crops, and livestock.

Practical ways to begin

  • Rotate crops: change the crop grown in a plot between seasons. Include legumes where they fit your farm plan.
  • Keep soil covered: use suitable mulch or crop residues to reduce bare soil and help conserve moisture. Balance this with the need for crop residues as livestock feed.
  • Return organic matter: use well-managed manure or compost to build soil health. Seek local advice on amounts and safe handling.
  • Protect water and soil: use locally suitable water-harvesting and erosion-control measures, especially on sloping land.
  • Mix crops and livestock carefully: plan fodder, manure, and grazing so animals do not damage crops or land.
  • Use local forecasts: adjust planting and field work to seasonal rainfall advice from county extension services.

Make a simple compost heap

Compost turns suitable plant material and farm waste into organic matter for the soil. Keep the heap where rainwater will not flood it, and ask an extension officer what materials are safe for your crops.

  1. Choose a shaded, well-drained spot and loosen a patch of soil about one metre square.
  2. Start with small twigs for airflow. Add alternating layers of dry plant material, green plant material, and a little topsoil or animal manure. Moisten each layer lightly.
  3. Build the heap to about shoulder height, then cover it with topsoil and dry grass or leaves. Keep it damp, not waterlogged.
  4. Check it regularly and turn the heap about every three weeks to help it decompose evenly.
  5. It may be ready after one to two months when the material is dark, crumbly, and no longer looks like the original scraps. Timing depends on the materials and weather.

Use well-decomposed compost and follow local advice on how much to apply. Do not assume that compost replaces every nutrient your crop may need.

Start with one change

  1. Choose a problem you want to improve, such as soil washing away or dry spells.
  2. Ask a local agricultural extension officer which practice suits your land.
  3. Try it on a small part of the farm first and keep simple notes about costs, labour, and results.
  4. Expand it if it works well for your farm.
